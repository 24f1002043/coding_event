    IV. Benford's test

    In honest financial records, about 30% of amounts start with a 1 and fewer than 5% start with a 9. Fabricated or manipulated books drift from this curve.

    V. Habit fingerprint

    When you usually spend, by weekday and hour. Darker ink means more spending at that time. Red rings mark a flagged entry, which usually falls outside your normal pattern.

    VII. Methods

    Robust outlier

    Median and median absolute deviation of log-amounts per category, so a single huge charge can't hide by inflating the average. Flags modified z > 3.5.

    Duplicate charge

    Same merchant and same amount within 48 hours (2 hours for small amounts).

    Split purchase

    Several same-day charges at one merchant, each under the ₹5,000 approval limit but adding up to more than it.

    Threshold hugging

    Amounts in the last 5% below the approval limit, a classic expense-fraud tell.

    Off-habit timing

    Large spends in hours that account for less than 2% of your history.

    Unknown payee

    First-ever merchant charging above your 90th percentile for that category. Only checked once there are 3 weeks of history to compare against.

    Category surge

    Last 30 days compared with the average of the three 30-day windows before them.

    Benford's law

    First-digit distribution tested with Pearson's χ² (8 df) against log₁₀(1 + 1/d).
